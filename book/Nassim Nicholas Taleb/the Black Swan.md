# Chapter 6 Narrative Fallacy 

## Definition 

Our brains don't accept the "randomness" or "events happened without any causes". They will compulsively make a series of events a story and add causation logic, making the world seemingly much more explainable and predictable.

People tend to believe a sentence with "reason". e.g. adding "because" at the end of the sentence.

## Narratives of National Characters 

"National Character" or "National Identity" lacks empirical validity that fails to explain or predict human behaviour. In contrast, gender, social class and profession are much more effective predictors of individual bahviour. A male from Sweden resembles a male from China much more than he does a female from Sweden.

You have a assorted group of rats, which are put in a radiation experiment. The weak ones, old ones died first and then there will progressively be stronger and stronger collection of rats, but note that the fact is that every single rat will be weaker after the radiation than before. # Chapter 10 The Scandal or Prediction 



## We are demonstrably arrogant about what we think we know.


A experiment on **epistemic arrogance (overconfidence)** asked participants to set an **interval range** for unknown facts (e.g., the number of Catherine II’s lovers) such that they were **98% confident** the true value fell within their range.

Instead of the expected 2% error rate, the actual error rate reached **45% among Harvard MBA students**, and typically ranged between **15% and 30% across various populations**. This demonstrates that people consistently overestimate the precision of their knowledge.


The implications of the arrogance for all the activities involving prediction.
