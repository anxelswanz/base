# 1. The beauty of the whole

> [!Just as if we were painting a statue and someone came up and blamed us, saying that we aren't putting the most beautiful colors on the most beautiful parts of the animal—for the eyes, which are the most beautiful part, are not painted purple but black—we would seem to make a reasonable defense to him by saying, 'Sir, don't suppose that we ought to paint eyes so beautiful that they don't even look like eyes, nor the other parts; but observe whether, by giving what's appropriate to each, we make the whole beautiful.]

> **著名的“雕像隐喻”：** 他把国家比作一座正在上色的雕像。有人批评说：“你为什么不把最美丽的紫色涂在最美丽的眼睛上，反而要把眼睛涂成黑色？” 苏格拉底回答：如果把眼睛装饰得太夸张，它就不再像眼睛了，整座雕像的美也就被破坏了。给每个部分它**固有的、自然的特质**，整体才会最美。

同样，如果让保卫者去享受黄金、美酒和情人，他们就不再是合格的保卫者，而会变成骄奢淫逸的寄生虫。


## 2.Four virtues 

2.1 Wisdom
Socrates' understanding of wisdom operates on two levels:
1. personal divine reflection, which manifests as the recognition of one's own ignorance.
2. Political foresight

2.2 Bravery
Socrates thinks bravery is perservation. 
When facing pleasure, pain, desire and fear, man's convictions never fade.

2.3 Temperance
 > [!"..within the man himself, in his soul, there is a **better part** and a **worse part**; and that when the **better part**, which is naturally smaller, **is master of the worse**, which is larger, this is called **‘being master of oneself’**, and it is an expression of praise...]
 
2.4 Justice （or Righteousness）

> [!that **each one man must perform one social service in the state for which his nature was best adapted**... And further, that **this doing one's own business and not being a busybody is justice**, this also we have heard from many others and have often said ourselves.]

我们必须让每一个人在城邦中担任一项最适合他天性的社会职务……而且，**这种各司其职、互不干涉（管闲事）的状态就是正义**。这一点我们不仅听别人说过，自己也经常挂在嘴边。

Righteousness is essentially the perservation of the three virtues above.

Socrates compared reason, desire, and spirit to three different musical notes, and justice is the harmony maintained among these three notes.

## The Degeneration of Regime 

1. [[Aristocracy]] 
	However, the political changes are all the results of the division within ruling classes.

2. Timocracy 
	Plato described it as a "degenerated" form of aristocracy. In this system, the rulers are not motivated by wisdom or the common good, but by ambition, military glory, and the pursuit of status. 
3. [[Oligarchy]] 
	The people who value glory or honour turn into those who value private property / money.
4. Democracy
	In Plato's view, democracy arises when the poor overcome the rich. While it initially appears attractive, he views it fundamentally unstable. 
		It treats all people and desires as equal, regardless of their wisdom, merit, meritocracy.
		Plato compared the democracy with a ship sailing in the sea where passengers fighting over a helm, completely ignoring the necessity of a skilled captain.
5. Tyranny 
	Plato believes that the extreme liberty inevitably leads to extreme servitude. When the liberty lacks the guidance and rational guidance, it degenerates anarchy. Demagogues confiscates the property of the rich under the pretext of championing the public interest.  They distributed mere pittance for the poor while pocketing the vast majority for themselves.
	自由导致失去秩序，而人们对混沌的承受度有限，当生活失去确定性的时候，人们会本能地去寻找秩序，哪怕是残酷的秩序。
