# Chapter 2 Forge (1750-1860)

Writing in 1845 in his investigation into the condition of the working-class in England, Friedrich Engels wrote from the perspective of middle-class that immorality in young people seems to be more prevalent in Sheffield than anywhere else. Young people spent their whole day doing pitch-penny and watching dogs fighting. At night, they will go to ale-house to drink and among the company were openly avowed prostitutes. 

The high wages that could be earned in cutlery industry in Sheffield, were in large part due to the dangers inherent in the job. And this is the actual cause of the perceived immorality. The cutlery grinders had an average life expectancy of under 35 years primarily due to the two severe hazards, that are first long hours of inhaling the silica dust, which destroy their lungs and the explosive grindstones. Despite this, it was often workers themselves who resisted the efforts to improve the working conditions and safety because they knew instinctively that this would make trades (行业/手艺) more accessible and drive down their wages.
