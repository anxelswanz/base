
# Pragmatism 

## The Definition of Pragmatism

### Charles Sanders Pierce

> The truth of an idea is found in its consequences.
> 
> “Let us illustrate this rule by some examples; and, to begin with the simplest one possible, let us ask what we mean by calling a thing _hard_. Evidently that it will not be scratched by many other substances.”


### John Dewey (1859—1952)

1. 思想是解决困境的工具

> “If ideas, meanings, conceptions, notions, theories, systems are instrumental to an active reorganization of the given environment, to a removal of some specific trouble and perplexity, then the test of their validity and value lies in accomplishing this work.”

如果观念、意义、概念、理论、体系都是用来主动重组环境、消除某种具体困扰的工具，那么检验它们是否有效、有价值，就看它们能否完成这项工作。

2. 民主是一种生活方式

他认为，民主和科学探究在精神上是一致的：

- 都不承认绝对权威，任何主张都要接受检验；

> “The man who wears the shoe knows best that it pinches and where it pinches, even if the expert shoemaker is the best judge of how the trouble is to be remedied.”

穿鞋的人最清楚鞋子哪里夹脚，即使鞋匠是修鞋的最佳专家。
                                                                             
- 都依赖开放的讨论和信息的自由流通；
- 都把结论视为可修正的，错了就改；
- 都把集体的经验和智慧当作解决问题的来源。

问题公开、讨论自由、证据说话、结论可改

《民主与教育》（1916）第七章
> “A democracy is more than a form of government; it is primarily a mode of associated living, of conjoint communicated experience.”

民主不只是一种政府形式，它首先是一种**联合生活的方式**，一种**共同交流的经验**。

同一章里，他提出衡量一个群体是否民主的两个标准：

> “How numerous and varied are the interests which are consciously shared? How full and free is the interplay with other forms of association?”

即：成员有意识地共享的利益有多少、有多丰富？这个群体与其他群体的互动有多充分、多自由？

> From pragmatist standpoint, truths outside of time are an illusory.

The idea of a timeless truth fails to distinguish what has happend from what might happen. (similar to [[Hume's Guillotine (is-ought problem)]])

What has happened has been validated through experiments, observation or experiences. But what might happen is just an assumption or an expectation. 

```
Take the proposition 'water boils at 100°C' as an example:

• What has occurred: In countless past experiments under standard atmospheric pressure, water boiled at 100°C.
• What is expected to occur: The next time water is heated, it will also boil at 100°C.

However, we know that at high altitudes, where atmospheric pressure changes, water no longer boils at 100°C.
```
