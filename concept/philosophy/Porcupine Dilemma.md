
The concept was created by German philosopher Arthur Schopenhauer in his 1851 book _Parerga and Paralipomena_.

- **The Story:** On a freezing winter day, a group of porcupines huddle together to share body heat and stay warm.
- **The Conflict:** As they press close, their sharp quills prick one another, causing pain and forcing them to move apart.
- **The Oscillation:** Feeling the cold again, they drift back together, only to poke each other once more.
- **The Resolution:** Through trial and error, they eventually find an optimal, moderate distance—close enough to share a bit of warmth, but far enough apart to avoid getting pricked.

Schopenhauer used the parable to show that human beings, driven by inner emptiness or loneliness, seek out companionship. However, because people have personal flaws, egos, and disagreeable traits, close contact inevitably leads to friction and emotional hurt.
