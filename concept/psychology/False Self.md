
The concept was developed by the British psychoanalyst Donal W. Winnicott. The concept describes a defensive mask that an individual constructs to navigate their environment, usually at the expense of their authentic emotions and spontaneity.

Winnicott observed that infants required "good enough" caring, where parents are attuned to children's spontaneous needs and expressions to develop "true self". When children's authentic expressions are consistently ignored, punished or met with demands for compliance, the child may develop a "False Self" to protect their True Self from being annihilated or rejected.

Winnicott was very scared of "good children", those whose parents couldn't tolerate to many bad behaviours and demand compliance too early and too strictly. This will lead, in Winnicott's formulation, to the emergence of "False Self", a persona that is outwardly good and outwardly compliant, but was suppressing its vital instinct.
