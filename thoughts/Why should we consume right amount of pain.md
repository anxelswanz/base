In today's world, it is a fact that it's easy to gain access to high-level release of dopamine, scrolling short form videos, partying, gaming, etc. It seems to us that pleasure is something we must have in our daily life. We tend to stay within the zone of joy and very reluctant to push ourselves to the other side of the scale, pain. 

However, what's the consequence of pleasure? Seneca， who was Roman Stoic philosopher, argued that, the more pleasure a man captures, the more master he will have to server. 

Anna Lembke, who is a prominent American psychaitrist specializing in addiction medcine, wrote in the book Dopamine Nations that our brains contain a balance, a scale, when we experience too much pleausure, the scale tips to the side of pleasure. The more our balance tips, and faster it tips, the more pleasure we feel. But our brains want homeostasis, which is a tendency for any living systems to achieve physiologic equilibrium. So the brain will trigger compensatory mechanism that suppresses pleasure. The book used a very interesting terms that these gremlins hop on the side of pain to counteract the weight of the pleasure side. However, these little gremlins don't get off once the scale reaches level, they remain on the scale until it tips to the side of the pain. That's the reason people experience comedown after being drunk overnight, and we feel anxiety or emptiness after scrolling phone or gaming all day. Opponent-Process Theory of Emotion and Motivation said that: any prolonged or repeated departures from hedonic and affective neutrality have a cost. 

But why should we go towards pain? 

Think about the runner's high. The process of running can be a bit challenging but after each run, we can feel that relaxing / calming state. The book Dopamine nations also used an example, after a patient overcame the addiction of alcohol and drugs, he struggled with severe emotional void and negative feeling. He discovered daily ice baths provided a controlled and temporary pain that relieve his distress, inducing a natural, euphoric state that proved essential to his recovery. As he said, the cold water reminds him of being alive can feel good. So with intermittent exposure to pain, the pleasure-pain balance gets weighted to the side of pleasure, such that we are much less vulnerable to pain and more able to feel pleasure over time.

Similarly, in the book ancient art of stoic joy, it wrote the stoics practiced a program that is called voluntary discomfort, that is welcoming a degree of discomfort in their life to increase their enjoyment of life. For example, stoics choose to be cold or hungry even though they could be warm and well-fed. In other words, voluntary discomfort can be thought of as a kind of vaccine, by exposing ourselves to a small amount of a weakened virus now, we create in ourselves an immunity that will protect us from a debilitating illness in the future. 

The book ancient art of stoic joy also argued that abstaining from pleasure can itself be pleasant, and it gave an example: that while on a diet, you develop a craving for the ice cream you know to be in your refrigerator. If you eat it, you will experience a certain gastronomic pleasure, along with a certain regret for having eaten it. If you refrain from eating the ice cream, though, you will forgo this gastronomic pleasure but will experience pleasure of a different kind: As Epictetus observes, you will “be pleased and will praise yourself ” for not eating it.

`When you have a vision of an attractive object, you must be careful not to be swept away by it... but say to yourself, ‘Wait for me a little; let me see what you are and what you represent; let me test you.’ ... **If you resist, what a sense of victory! What a sense of self-approval!**`
