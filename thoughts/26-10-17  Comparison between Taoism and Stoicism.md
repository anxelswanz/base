

## Turning difficulties, problems, obstacles into opportunity, inspiration

In book 4 of the Meditations by Marcus Aurelius, he said the directing mind, that is the rational, ruling centre of the human minds, has no favoured material for its work [[Mediations#Adaptability]] . This means no matter what condition the thing is, favourable circumstances or adversity, the directing mind acts the same as usual and it possesses the ability of turning adversity into its own use.

It echoes the eastern philosophy for example there's a Chinese saying from Taoism, it goes: Misery, bless is found by its side. Good fortune, misery lurks beneath it. That is also called the Blessing in disguise. 

## Be like water, be like fire

In Marcus' opinion, a man's heart should be a bright big fire that devours everything and turns everything into his own fuel instead of being a small flame that can be quickly extinguished by an object a person throws upon it. In Taoism. It is said that the highest good is like water (上善若水). People should learn from the water as it flows in silence nourishes everyone and asks nothing for return.  

The fire Marcus used in Meditations is different as fire here is seen as assimilation and more active. It actively devours everything and makes its flame to be higher. The concept of water in Taoism represents the kind of de-escalation that can be understood as competing with no one  as well as the tolerance as it can be in any shape and being tolerant towards external environment. It represents the core idea of Taoism, that is governance with non-action. 

## Tao and LOGOS

Both sides have the concept of a fundamental principle that governs the universe. In Taoism, that name is called Tao, which can be understood as fundamental principle that guides the universe. In Stoicism, it's called LOGOS, or nature, and it was proposed by Heraclitus. It means the universal law that guides the universe. 

## Compliance with nature 

In Taoism, the statement goes "Man follows the way of earth, the earth follows the way of heaven, heaven follows the way of Tao, Tao follows the way of its own nature" (人法地，地法天，天法道，道法自然). The nature here means one's own self-being or spontaneity. It basically says even the fundamental principle is following one's own nature, this also shows the idea of Taoism that men should let the nature take its course. For Stoicism, Marcus said similar thing,  "if you expect nothing, shirk nothing but self-content with each present action taken in accordance nature and a heroic truthfulness in all that you say and mean - then you will lead a good life" (3.12). 

The difference here between two  philosophy is the compliance with  nature. In Stoicism, it tells people to use rational judgement to comply with the nature  [[Mediations#Revere your power of judgements]] while in Taoism, people are advised to let go and allow things to take their own course without force. Beyond that, Stoicism asks for action after rational judgement, it requires men to take on responsibility and live up to their own duty. This can be seen from that fact that Marcus Aurelius, as an emperor and philosopher, still led the troops into battles. Whereas Taoist thought believes people should govern without  unnatural interference. It tends towards withdrawal and detachment.
