

# The Newcomen Engine (1712)


It was invented by Thomas Newcomen for pumping the water out of coal mines. The steam was injected into a cylinder to push a piston up, and then cold water was sprayed directly inside the cylinder to condense the steam, This created a vacuum inside, allowing normal atmospheric pressure to push the piston back down.

# Spinning Jenny (1764)

Although early Spinning Jennies were mainly powered by human labour and kept in home workshops, as the machines grew in size, private households could no longer accommodate them. This laid the groundwork for the true 'Factory System' that followed.


# James Watt's refinement of Newcomen Engine (1765 - 1788)

Because cold water was sprayed into the cylinder to cool down the exhaust steam, the active steam coming next will need to heat the cylinder all over again, this resulted in a waste of huge amount of fuel. Up to 80% to 90% of steam was wasted just reheating cylinder metal.

### 1. Two Separate Engines
Watt then refined the engine by using separate two engines. Watt redesigned it by injecting two steams alternatively above and below the piston.


### 2. Rotary Motion 

The idea comes from William Murdoch (an employee of Boulton & Watt) "Sun and Planet" system. the underlying geometry of the Sun and Planet gear—known as **planetary gearing**—became foundational to how cars shift gears



# Slavery in the first industrial revolution

During the early decades of the First Industrial Revolution (1760 - 1807), British individuals and companies directly bought enslaved people to work on colonial plantations. Even after the UK banned the buying of enslaved people in 1807 and abolished slavery in 1833, British industrial factories continued to rely heavily on raw materials produced by enslaved labour in other countries.

## 1. Timeline

- **1760–1807 (Active Buying):** British traders were world leaders in the Transatlantic Slave Trade. British merchants bought millions of enslaved Africans and shipped them to British colonies in the Caribbean and North America.
    
- **1807 (Trade Outlawed):** Parliament passed the _Slave Trade Act_, making it illegal for British ships to buy, transport, or sell enslaved people.
    
- **1833 (Slavery Outlawed):** Parliament passed the _Slavery Abolition Act_, banning the ownership of enslaved people throughout most of the British Empire.


## 2. How slavery fueled the first Industrial Revolution 

The British Industrial Revolution was driven above all by <u>textile industries</u> (especially manchester). British factories relied heavily on <u>raw cotton</u> produced by enslaved labour in the Caribbean. Without cheap, slave-produced cotton, the rapid expansion of British mechanized mills would not have been possible at that scale or speed.


> `What share of Britain's total economic output did the textile industry represent during the First Industrial Revolution?`
> At its peak in the 1830s, the broader textile sector (cotton, wool, linen, and silk) generated **roughly 45% to 50% of Britain's total manufacturing output** and accounted for **12% to 15% of Britain's total GDP**.]

<u>Profits</u> made from buying enslaved people generated vast wealth in the UK. Financial institutions like Barclays, Lloyds and the Bank of England had strong historical roots toed slave-trade profits.
