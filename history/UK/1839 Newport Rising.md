
**1. Background & Triggers**

- **Rejection of the First Petition**: In July 1839, the House of Commons decisively rejected the first Chartist petition signed by over 1.2 million people.
    
- **Arrests of Leaders**: Disillusionment spread quickly, leading to nationwide unrest. When key local Chartist figures (like Henry Vincent) were jailed in Monmouth, radicals in South Wales decided that peaceful petitioning ("moral force") had failed and armed rebellion ("physical force") was necessary.
    
- **Economic Despair**: Ironworkers and coal miners in the South Wales Valleys faced brutal working conditions, low pay, and exploitation via the "truck system" (being paid in tokens only redeemable at high-priced company stores).
    

**2. The March on Newport**

- **The Plan**: Led by **John Frost** (a former mayor of Newport turned Chartist leader), along with **Zephaniah Williams** and **William Jones**, the Chartists planned to march three separate columns of armed men into Newport overnight on November 3.
    
- **Objectives**: Their goal was to capture Newport, liberate imprisoned Chartist leaders, and potentially trigger a wider general insurrection across Great Britain.
    
- **The Storm**: Torrential rain and logistical delays disrupted the plan. The columns failed to merge on time, delaying their arrival until Monday morning, November 4.
    

**3. The Clash at the Westgate Hotel**

- **The Standoff**: Around 3,000 to 5,000 Chartists, armed with pikes, muskets, sticks, and axes, marched into the town center and gathered outside the **Westgate Hotel**, where local magistrates were holding captured Chartist prisoners under the guard of about 30 soldiers (the 45th Regiment of Foot) and special constables.
    
- **The Bloody Outcome**: A confrontation broke out at the hotel doors. When Chartists breached the building, soldiers inside fired point-blank into the crowd through the windows.
    
- **Casualties**: The battle lasted only about 20 minutes. At least **22 Chartists were killed** (and over 50 wounded), while several soldiers and constables were injured. The crowd scattered in panic.
    

**4. Aftermath & Historical Legacy**

- **Trial for High Treason**: Government forces reacted swiftly with widespread arrests. John Frost, Zephaniah Williams, and William Jones were convicted of high treason and initially sentenced to be **hanged, drawn, and quartered**—the last people in Britain ever to receive that sentence.
    
- **Commutation to Transportation**: Fearing widespread public uproar and martyr-making, the British government commuted their sentences to **life transportation to Van Diemen's Land** (modern-day Tasmania). Frost was eventually pardoned in 1856 and returned to Britain.
    
- **Historical Significance**: The crushing defeat of the Newport Rising temporarily devastated the "physical force" wing of Chartism, shifting the movement back toward organization, education, and mass petitioning under leaders like Feargus O'Connor.
