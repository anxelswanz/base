
A group of English elites secretly invited a foreign ruler to invade their country, forcing the King to flee in disgrace.

James II was a devout Roman Catholic in a fiercely Protestant country. James II repeatedly bypassed Parliament, suspended laws protecting Protestants and tried to centralize power under the Crown, threatening the authority of English noblemen. A group of English nobles secretly invited William of Orange (a Protestant Dutch prince) married to James II's Protestant daughter, Mary. James II's military officers, allies and even his other daughter (Anne) deserted him to support William and Marry when William landed in England with a massive Dutch army. James II fled to exile in France.

18th-centuary historians dubbed it "Glorious" primarily because it was seen as nearly bloodless coup in England.
