
Before William III and Mary II were officially crowned as co-monarchs, Parliament required them to accept the **Bill of Rights 1689**. This legal document permanently altered the balance of power in Britain by establishing that:

- **Parliament is Supreme:** The monarch could no longer suspend or create laws, levy taxes, or maintain a standing army during peacetime without Parliament’s explicit consent.

- **Regular Parliaments:** Parliament had to be held frequently, and elections were to be free.

- **Freedom of Speech:** Free speech within parliamentary debates was legally protected from royal interference.


The Bill of Rights and [[1688 Glorious Revolution (UK)]] marked that the UK entered [[Parliamentary Monarchy]].
