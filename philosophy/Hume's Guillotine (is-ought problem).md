
Articulated by the 18th-century Scottish philosopher David Hume, the idea acts as a "guillotine" by severing descriptive factual claims from prescriptive moral conclusions. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is%E2%80%93ought_problem), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UwkSA8nqmdI&t=105), [3](http://www.philosophy-index.com/hume/guillotine/)]

```
We cannot derive 'moral values' (judgments) purely and directly from 'objective facts' (descriptions).
```

我们无法纯粹从“客观事实”中直接推导（描述）出“道德价值”（评判）。

For example, why people believe there's going to be a sunrise tomorrow?
Because in the past millions and billions years, the sun rose every single day. But actually, there's a hidden premise, that is people assume the past will be the same as the future. But there could be a sudden lose of gravity or sun exploded that could entirely break this belief.
Therefore induction reasoning cannot be rationally proven.
